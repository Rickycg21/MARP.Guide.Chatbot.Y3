## **2. How is my overall mark for a postgraduate taught module calculated?**

**Answer:**  
Your overall mark for a postgraduate taught module is calculated by averaging the percentage marks from each component assessment and then converting this average into an aggregate score. This computation may employ weights as specified in the course documentation, and the final aggregation score is used to determine your overall mean and degree classification [1][2]. [3]

## **5. How is progression determined from one stage of a postgraduate programme to the next?**

**Answer:**

Additionally, departments must clearly communicate academic expectations and requirements for progression to students [2], while also providing guidance on module selection and academic opportunities [3].

## **6. What are the requirements for receiving a postgraduate certificate or diploma?**

## **9. How are extenuating circumstances handled under the postgraduate assessment regulations?**

**Answer:**  
Extenuating circumstances are handled by departmental Exceptional Circumstances Committees (ECC), which review claims supported by written evidence to determine if the circumstances adversely affected a student's academic performance. If validated, the ECC can propose remedies to the examination board, such as allowing a further attempt at an exam or the opportunity to retake modules [1][2]. It's important to note that all claims must be considered post hoc, meaning they are evaluated after the submission deadline [1][2]. [3]

## **13. Are there specific regulations about group work and how it is assessed?**

**Answer:**  
Group work assessment is subject to specific regulations. In cases of group submissions, all students within the group must sign a declaration statement affirming their individual contributions and that the work is original [3]. Furthermore, any group assessment that involves multiple markers must follow transparent processes for resolving grading disagreements, including potential third-party adjudication [1]. The marking for group assessments is also required to undergo calibration and moderation procedures to ensure fairness across different markers [2].
